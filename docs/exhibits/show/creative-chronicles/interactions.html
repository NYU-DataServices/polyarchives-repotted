Interactions & Collaborations

New Management

An announcement was featured in an issue of the Reporter, dated October 12, 1967. The article sheds light on the influential role of Golana magazine within the Polytechnic community, particularly through the election of Carl T. Grasso and Gilbert L. Wachsman as Editors-in-Chief for the upcoming year.

Polytechnic Reporter, Vol. 60, No. 2, Page 7, 10/12/1967

Golana, now in its fifth year of publication, continues to uphold its tradition of excellence in science fiction literature, solidifying its reputation as a pioneering force in the literary landscape.

Another Scene from Tolkien's Middle Earth (1/2)

The appointment of Grasso and Wachsman as the new Editors-in-Chief shows the magazine's desire for continuously fostering talent and innovation within its leadership.

Grasso brings a wealth of creative vision and technical expertise to his role. His story, "The Time Machine Revisited," showcases his talent and imagination, resonating with the captivating narratives that Golana is known for.

Likewise, Wachsman's extensive experience in various publications, including the Reporter and the Engineer, positions him as a valuable asset to Golana's editorial team.

His familiarity with the intricacies of campus journalism and his involvement in "certain less overt ventures" suggests a depth of insight and resourcefulness that bodes well for the magazine's future endeavors.

Another Scene from Tolkien's Middle Earth (2/2)

Furthermore, the article highlights the diverse talents and contributions of Golana's staff members, each bringing their unique skills and perspectives to the publication.

From Allan E. Levy's managerial prowess to John Hoffman's technical expertise, Golana's editorial team effectively collaborates with one another, reflecting the magazine's broader mission of nurturing creative expression and experimentation.

The announcement also underscores Golana's continued evolution and growth, with improvements in technical aspects such as linotype copy, editorials, and book reviews, as well as a professional layout. This pursuit of excellence is further evidenced by its aspirations to win a Hugo award.

Golana Issue 12 Cover

Moreover, the article serves as an open invitation for students, particularly freshmen, to become actively involved in Golana's editorial process. By providing opportunities for students to gain hands-on experience and contribute to the creative vision of the magazine, Golana fosters a sense of community and collaboration that transcends disciplinary boundaries and enriches the campus culture at Polytechnic Institute.

Among Others

One article from the Reporter, dated September 19, 1968, offers a comprehensive overview of Polytechnic's rich literary tradition, showcasing the enduring legacy of other publications that have shaped the university's cultural landscape.

Within this vibrant ecosystem of creativity and expression, Golana magazine occupies a prominent place, alongside other esteemed publications such as the Reporter, the Engineer, and Counterweight Quarterly.

Polytechnic Reporter, Vol. 61, No. 1, Page 4, 9/19/1968

Polytechnic publications encompass a diverse array of literary outlets, each offering unique opportunities for students to engage in writing, photography, and magazine layout. Golana, however, stands out as a bastion of imaginative storytelling at this time.

Despite its modest frequency of publication, Golana manages to mantain an enduring presence within Polytechnic's literary tradition. This is evidenced by its inclusion alongside other longstanding publications such as the Reporter and the Engineer.

Moreover, the invitation extended to students to become involved in Golana's editorial process speaks to the magazine's continued commitment to inclusivity and mentorship. Aspiring writers and artists are encouraged to join Golana's ranks, contributing their talents to the ongoing legacy of the publication. In this way, Golana serves as a nurturing ground for emerging talent.
